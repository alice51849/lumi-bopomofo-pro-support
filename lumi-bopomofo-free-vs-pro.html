When the free Lumi Bopomofo is enough

  • Your child is just meeting Bopomofo and you want to see whether the feed-the-friend and tracing games hold their attention before spending anything.
  • You mainly want recognition and tracing practice; the free listing covers listening, picking the symbol, tracing strokes, the four tones and blending on the Sound Train.
  • You care about the basics every parent cares about, and the free listing already promises them: completely ad-free, no personal data, no sign-up, progress stored only on the device.
  • You want an English and Chinese interface so a non-Mandarin-speaking parent can navigate it.

When Lumi Bopomofo Pro is worth paying for

  • You want the whole thing on day one. The Pro listing says all 37 sounds, every level and every game are unlocked with one purchase.
  • Your child is past recognition. Bopomofo Fishing (listen to the blend, catch the right fish) and Star Race (beat the clock) are the two games only the Pro listing names, and both are about blending and speed.
  • You want to see progress without sitting beside them. Daily goals, weak spot review and a parents page are listed for Pro.
  • No Wi-Fi where they play. The Pro listing says no internet is required, which matters in the car, on a plane to Taiwan, or on a classroom iPad.
  • Several kids, several devices. It is a universal iPhone and iPad app the listing says you can share with the whole family.
  • You are a weekend Chinese school teacher. A buy-once app with no in-app purchase pop-ups, no outside links and no social features is easier to put on a recommended list than anything with an unknown upgrade path.

Moving from free to Pro

Both listings say progress stays on the device and neither mentions an account, so there is nothing to migrate: treat Pro as a fresh start. Children at this age rarely mind replaying the first levels, and the Pro weak spot review will quickly settle on the sounds that still need work. If your child has favourite friends in the free app, keep it installed; the two apps live side by side.

Whichever you choose, the routine around the app matters more than the app: ten to fifteen minutes on school nights, one final at a time for blending, all five tones in a row for pitch, and two new symbols traced per week.
